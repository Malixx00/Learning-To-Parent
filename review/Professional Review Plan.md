## 1. What this is

The Parenting Toolkit is a small app Liam built for himself and friends. It covers parenting from pregnancy to adult children. It isn't a public or commercial product yet. Before sharing it more widely, Liam wants professionals to check that nothing in it is **wrong, out of date, unsafe, or misleading**.

The content was written from:
- official sources (e.g. Red Nose, ASCIA, NHMRC, WHO, health.gov.au, eSafety, CDC)
- research papers
- well-known parenting books
- a podcast series

Every card is a paraphrased summary, not the original source.

**You aren't expected to rewrite anything.** Just flag what's wrong and, if you can, suggest a fix and a source.

## 2. How the app is laid out (5-minute tour)

1. Open the app link. Along the top are **7 stages**: Expecting, Newborn (0–3 months), Baby (3–12 months), Toddler (1–3), Kids (3–12), Tweens and teens (12–19), Young adults (20+).
2. Each stage has **cards**. There are two kinds:
   - **Health guides** (marked "Health guide", shaded pink in the workbook). These summarise official health, safety or legal guidance. Each has:
     - The official guidance
     - For the partner
     - Get help when
     - Check the source (links)
     - A "Last checked" date
   - **Advice cards**: parenting situations (e.g. tantrums, bullying, staying connected with a teen). Each has:
     - What's likely going on
     - Say / Avoid
     - What to do
     - Why it works
     - Try this week
     - Sources
3. **Practice** (top menu): multiple-choice scenarios. Each has three options, and the app marks one as the **best answer** and explains why each option is or isn't best.
4. **Get help** (top menu): Australian phone lines and crisis steps (a baby who won't stop crying; worried about your teen).
5. **Today**: a daily tip (no need to review separately; the tips come from the cards).

## 4. Step-by-step method

Work through your rows in the **Checklist** sheet, top to bottom.

**For each card:**

1. Click **Open card** in the workbook. It opens that card in the app.
2. Read the whole card once without stopping.
3. Go through it a second time with the checklist in section 5 (Health guide or Advice card).
4. Log every problem in the **Issues** sheet, **one row per problem** (see section 6).
5. Set **Review status** in the Checklist:
   - *Reviewed - no issues*
   - *Reviewed - issues logged*
   - *Not my area*: you started it but it's outside your expertise.
6. Add your initials.

**Then the practice scenarios:**

7. Go to the **Scenarios** sheet and filter by the cards you reviewed. Each row shows the situation, the app's best answer, the other two options and the app's reason.
8. For each one, set **Agree with best answer?** to *Yes*, or to *No* or *Unsure* and log an issue explaining why.

**Finally:**

9. If you're a GP, nurse, midwife or counsellor, check the **Get help page** (row H-001): phone numbers, hours, who each line is for, and the crisis steps.
10. Fill in your name, profession and date on the **Start here** sheet, then send the workbook back to Liam.

## 5. What to check

### A. Health guides (pink rows)

| ✓ | Check |
|---|---|
| ☐ | **Correct:** each statement in "The official guidance" matches current Australian guidance. |
| ☐ | **Current:** nothing has been superseded (guidelines, schedules, laws, product rules). |
| ☐ | **Australian:** right for Australia, or clearly says "check your state" where laws differ. |
| ☐ | **Numbers:** doses, ages, hours, amounts and timeframes are right. |
| ☐ | **Safety-critical:** nothing that could lead to harm if a parent followed it exactly. |
| ☐ | **Get help when:** the thresholds for seeing a GP, calling a helpline or calling 000 are right, and nothing important is missing. |
| ☐ | **Nothing important left out:** a key warning, exception or group (e.g. premature babies, medical conditions) that should be mentioned. |
| ☐ | **Links:** each link opens and supports what the card says. |
| ☐ | **Plain English:** a tired parent would understand it and not misread it. |

### B. Advice cards

| ✓ | Check |
|---|---|
| ☐ | **Consistent with evidence:** the advice fits current research and good practice. |
| ☐ | **"What's likely going on":** the explanation of the child's behaviour or development is accurate for the age. |
| ☐ | **Say / Avoid:** the phrases are appropriate; nothing in "Say" could cause harm; nothing in "Avoid" is actually fine. |
| ☐ | **Steps:** the steps are safe, realistic and in a sensible order. |
| ☐ | **"Why it works":** the claim isn't overstated, and the research is described fairly. |
| ☐ | **Age fit:** the advice suits the age range of the stage. |
| ☐ | **Red flags:** where needed, the card says when to get professional help (and doesn't treat a sign of a disorder, disability or abuse as ordinary behaviour). |
| ☐ | **Inclusive:** works for different family types, cultures, neurodivergent children and children with disability, or at least doesn't exclude them. |
| ☐ | **Tone:** non-judgmental and not shaming of parents or children. |

### C. Practice scenarios

| ✓ | Check |
|---|---|
| ☐ | The **best answer** really is the best of the three for that situation. |
| ☐ | The **"why"** explanations are accurate and fair (a "wrong" option isn't actually reasonable in some cases). |
| ☐ | The situation is realistic for the age given. |
| ☐ | Nothing in any option is dangerous without the explanation making that clear. |

### D. Get help page

| ✓ | Check |
|---|---|
| ☐ | Every phone number is correct. |
| ☐ | Hours and age ranges for each service are correct. |
| ☐ | The crisis steps (crying baby; worried about your teen) are safe and complete. |
| ☐ | Any important service is missing (e.g. one specific to your field). |

## 6. How to log an issue

Use the **Issues** sheet, **one row per problem**, even if a card has several.

| Column | What to put |
|---|---|
| **Ref** | The card's Ref from the Checklist (e.g. `C-011`), the scenario's Ref (e.g. `S-042`), or `H-001` for the Get help page. |
| **Card** | The card title. |
| **Section** | Pick from the list: What's going on, Say, Avoid, Steps, Why it works, Tip, Guidance, For the partner, Get help when, Links, Scenario, Help page, Other. |
| **Severity** | See below. |
| **Current text** | Copy the exact words from the app, so it can be found. |
| **What's wrong** | A short explanation. |
| **Suggested change** | Your suggested wording, if you have one. "Remove this" is fine too. |
| **Evidence / source** | The guideline, website, paper or professional standard (with year or page if you can). "Clinical experience" is fine. |
| **Reviewer / Date** | Your initials and the date. |

Leave **Status** and **Liam's notes** blank; Liam uses those to track fixes.

### Severity

| Severity | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Critical** | Could lead to harm if followed. Must be fixed before the app is shared. | Unsafe sleep advice; wrong emergency number; missing "call 000" for a red-flag symptom. |
| **Major** | Factually wrong, out of date, or not right for Australia. | An outdated vaccine schedule; a wrong age or dose; a law stated as national when it varies by state. |
| **Minor** | Unclear, incomplete or could be misread. | A step that could be read two ways; a missing caveat that matters for some families. |
| **Suggestion** | Wording, tone, or something worth adding. | A better phrase; a topic that should have a card. |

### Example

| Ref | Card | Section | Severity | Current text | What's wrong | Suggested change | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-011 | Starting solids and allergies | Get help when | Minor | "Hives, swelling of face/lips/eyes or vomiting after a food: stop that food and see your GP." | Could also mention an ASCIA action plan once an allergy is diagnosed. | Add: "If an allergy is diagnosed, ask for an ASCIA Action Plan." | ASCIA website, 2026 |

*(This is only an example of the format.)*

## 7. Sending it back

- Save the workbook and email it back to Liam.
- If you'd prefer to talk something through, note "Needs discussion" in **What's wrong** and Liam will call you.
- The **Summary** sheet counts your issues by severity automatically.

**What happens next:** Liam will fix every Critical and Major issue before the app is shared further, and record what was done in the Status column. You're welcome to see the updated version.

## 8. A note on your role

This is a voluntary, informal check for a personal project shared with friends. Your review isn't a formal endorsement and you're not taking on responsibility for the app. The app already tells users it's not medical or professional advice and to check with their GP, midwife or child health nurse. If you'd like to be credited (or not) once it's checked, just let Liam know.
